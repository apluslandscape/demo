Contracts

Thursday 15 October 2026

Every recurring agreement A-Plus holds is recorded here, and the page follows the work in the order it happens: invoices that are due are drafted first, contracts that have lapsed or are ending soon are renewed next, and the full register sits below both.

Each step is carried out by a person. The system assembles a draft and shows the work behind every line, but nothing is created in QuickBooks and nothing is sent to a customer until someone has reviewed it and chosen to go ahead. The strip below shows where things currently stand; selecting a segment moves to that part of the page.

Step 1

Due to invoice

Recurring invoices whose period has closed, oldest first. Each entry shows the amount assembled from the contract’s terms and the work recorded against it. Selecting Review and send opens the draft, where every line can be checked against the visits that produced it before anything is created in QuickBooks.

More about this step

How an amount is worked out. A contract billed at a flat monthly rate produces a single line for the period. A contract billed for each service produces one line for each approved visit, priced from the rate recorded on the contract. Anything found in the period that the contract does not cover appears separately as an extra, with the visit that produced it beside it.

Only approved work is billed. A visit that has not yet been approved appears on the draft but is not included in the amount. Two visits recorded as an overlapping pair are held back until someone confirms which of them is real.

Contracts whose paperwork has lapsed still appear here. An account that is still being serviced is still owed an invoice, so a lapsed contract is drafted as usual and marked, rather than quietly left out of the run.

Nothing to bill and terms not sufficient are different things. The first means the period genuinely produced no billable work. The second means the contract does not yet carry enough information for an amount to be worked out, and the missing terms need to be added to the contract before it can be drafted.

Amounts are worked out from the contract, never estimated. Where a figure came from the office contract spreadsheet rather than a signed document it is marked as unconfirmed, and the review screen repeats that before anything is created.

Step 2

Renewals

Contracts that have already lapsed, followed by contracts ending within the next 180 days. Renewing a contract creates a new one that begins where the current one ends, with the current terms carried across and any agreed annual increase already applied as a suggestion.

More about this step

A lapsed contract is not closed on our side. It stays on file and keeps being invoiced while the account is being serviced, because the alternative is an account quietly leaving the invoicing run and the work going unbilled.

A contract that has been signed but has not yet started is listed separately. It is not yet in force, so it is not invoiced and does not appear under Due to invoice until its start date passes.

The current contract is kept when a renewal is created. The renewal is created as a draft; the contract it replaces is marked as replaced once the renewal is signed, and both remain readable afterwards.

How far ahead this looks. Contracts ending within the next 180 days are listed. That period can be changed, and a contract with no end date recorded does not appear here at all.

Reference

All contracts

Every contract on file, including those that have expired, been replaced or been cancelled. Contracts are grouped by account, since an account may hold more than one at the same time.

More about this section

What each status means. A contract is a draft while it is being written, sent once it has gone to the customer, and signed when it comes back. It becomes active on its start date and expired after its end date, unless a renewal has taken its place, in which case it is marked replaced. Cancelled is used when an agreement ends before its term does.

Where a figure came from. A figure taken from the office contract spreadsheet is marked as unconfirmed until it has been checked against the signed document. Unconfirmed figures are still used to assemble invoices, and the review screen says so before anything is created.

When two contracts disagree. Where more than one contract on an account describes the same terms, the disagreement is recorded and shown rather than resolved. Someone decides which contract governs; the system does not choose one for them.

Contracts and the rates in Yeti. Each service line on a contract is matched to the service the crew selects in Yeti. Where the rate recorded on the contract and the rate set up in Yeti differ, the contract is the authority and the difference is flagged.
