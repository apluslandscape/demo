A-Plus Lawn Care · Lead area

Every inquiry lives on this page from the moment it arrives until it becomes a paying customer or is given a recorded ending. The three stacks answer one question at a glance: whose move is next. Awaiting Review holds arrivals nobody has looked at yet, Action Required means the next move is ours, and Awaiting Customer means we are waiting on them.

The person who has waited longest sits at the top of every stack, so the work always starts at the top. This page never contacts anyone on its own - every reply, logged conversation, and recorded ending is something one of us chose to do - and it keeps itself up to date, so there is never anything to refresh.

How this page works

Why it matters

Every open lead lives on this one screen, whatever door it came in through - a call, a text, an email, Facebook, or a conversation in person. Each row answers the two questions that matter: what is waiting, and for how long. Nothing sits out of view, and nothing leaves the list without a recorded reason.

Where to start

When Awaiting Review is showing, start there. It holds inquiries that arrived on their own and have not been looked at yet, and its job is to reach zero. Open each one, read what came in, and place it: answering the person, logging the first call, or scheduling the estimate puts it in the right stack on its own, and Set the status under More covers anything unusual. Then work Action Required from the top - the oldest wait first.

The three stacks

A lead's position is its meaning. Action Required means the ball is with us: first contact not yet made, an estimate to schedule, write, or send, or a customer reply waiting on an answer. Awaiting Customer means the ball is with them: we have answered, or the estimate is in their hands, and the days figure shows how long they have been quiet - a row that sits there a while is the signal to nudge, or to record an ending. Awaiting Review appears only when something new has arrived.

What each phrase means

Awaiting initial contact - a new lead nobody has reached out to yet.

Estimate scheduled - a time is set to go look at the work.

Estimate in progress - the estimate is being written and is not finished.

Estimate not sent - the estimate is finished but has never gone out.

Customer reply received - the customer has responded and is awaiting our answer.

Awaiting reply - we made the most recent contact and are awaiting the customer's response.

Estimate sent - the estimate is in the customer's hands, awaiting their decision.

What each action does

Tapping a row opens the whole story: every email, logged conversation, note, and estimate event in one timeline, newest first, with unread items marked. From there, Reply sends an email and moves the lead to Awaiting Customer. Log call-text records a conversation that already happened - and because who contacted whom is what moves a lead between stacks, the log is the update: logging that the customer reached us places the lead in Action Required, and logging that we reached them places it in Awaiting Customer. Flag asks Paul, Jen, or Mike to look at something; it lands on that person's task list. Under More: add a note, create a task, set the status, enroll email history, or record an ending.

Endings and the record

One ending is automatic: when a deposit is paid, the lead becomes a customer and leaves this list on its own. Every other ending is deliberate: Record an ending files the lead under the Record view with a category and one line of what happened, and nothing about it is final - a finished lead's panel offers Reopen, which returns it to the working list with its whole story intact.

The working rhythm

Day to day, conversations go on the record as they happen - a call, a text, or a conversation in person is logged while it is fresh. A couple of times a week, someone walks the whole page top to bottom: Awaiting Review to zero, Action Required oldest first - acting on each row or turning it into a task - then Awaiting Customer oldest first, nudging the quiet ones or recording an ending, and clearing anything unread along the way.

What happens on its own - and what does not

Two things happen by themselves: estimate states keep themselves current - writing, finishing, or sending an estimate updates the row without anyone touching it - and a paid deposit converts the lead to a customer. Everything else is a person's deliberate act. This page never emails, texts, or calls a customer on its own, and it never closes a lead on its own. When something new arrives from a customer, the unread marker is the page asking a person to look - not the page acting for them.
